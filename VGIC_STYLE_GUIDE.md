# VGIC Style Guide — Tone, Structure & Default Positions

*Condensed reference for drafting new VGIC regulatory comments. Derived from 297 public VGIC filings, 2020–2026. Not a legal or factual authority — verify docket numbers, current standards, and program details before filing.*

## Who VGIC Is

VGIC is a 501(c)(6) trade association of EV OEMs and vehicle-grid integration (VGI) technology/charging companies. Standard self-description to use in any filing to a body VGIC hasn't filed with before:

> "The Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (VGIC) is a 501(c)(6) membership-based trade association committed to advancing the role of electric vehicles (EVs) and vehicle-grid integration (VGI) through policy development, education, outreach, and research."

## Voice and Tone

- **Collaborative advocate, not adversarial litigant.** Even when opposing a utility or Commission proposal outright, frame VGIC as a technical partner helping reach a better outcome, not an opponent. Disagree directly but briefly, then pivot immediately to a constructive alternative.
- **Hedge with purpose.** Default verbs: "VGIC believes," "VGIC recommends," "VGIC respectfully requests," "VGIC appreciates." Reserve "VGIC strongly supports/recommends/urges/opposes" for genuinely high-priority positions — don't overuse "strongly" or it loses force.
- **State firm positions as short, direct topic sentences** — optionally in bold or ALL CAPS as a pseudo-heading — even inside an otherwise measured paragraph, e.g. "VGIC OPPOSES [X]" or "VGIC RECOMMENDS THE COMMISSION [Y]." Follow immediately with the reasoning.
- **Cite everything.** Footnote CPUC/PUC decisions, advice letters, prior VGIC comments, other parties' comments, and technical standards (UL 1741, IEEE 1547). The register is policy-technical and legal, never political or promotional.
- **Recurring framing vocabulary** to reach for: "no-regrets" (low-risk regardless of how the proceeding resolves), "voluntary" / "a carrot rather than a stick" (opposing mandates, especially for load management), "technology-neutral," "narrowly tailored," "flexible grid resource" (frame EVs as a grid asset, not just new load).
- **Address regulators deferentially and specifically** — name the ALJ/Commissioner/Secretary, cite the specific ruling being responded to. **Address utilities by name and specific advice-letter/exhibit number**, professionally, even in disagreement — never generalize a criticism into an attack on the utility's motives.

## Structural Patterns

**Opening — pick based on venue:**
- *CPUC formal filings* (comments/reply comments/briefs/testimony): CPUC caption block, then an all-caps title ("COMMENTS OF THE VEHICLE-GRID INTEGRATION COUNCIL ON [subject]").
- *CPUC advice letter responses/protests*: business letter to "CPUC Energy Division Tariff Unit," invoking General Order 96-B, "Re: Response/Protest of the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council to Advice Letter [#] of [Utility]."
- *Other states/agencies/federal*: short "Introduction" section stating the docket, then the standard self-description boilerplate above, then substance.

**Body:** Roman-numeral sections for longer filings (I. Introduction, II. Background, III–IV substantive sections by issue, V. Conclusion). Each substantive section typically opens with a bolded/capitalized position statement, then 1–3 paragraphs of supporting reasoning with footnoted citations. Shorter letters use simple paragraph flow without numbered sections.

**Closing — nearly universal two-part pattern:**
1. A forward-looking appreciation line, near-verbatim across the corpus: *"VGIC appreciates the opportunity to [submit/provide] these [comments/reply comments] and looks forward to further collaboration with the Commission and stakeholders on this [initiative/proceeding]."* Swap in the agency/utility name as needed. **This is VGIC's single most identifiable stylistic signature — use some form of it at the end of every filing.**
2. "Respectfully submitted" (formal filings) or "Sincerely" (letters), name, title, "Vehicle-Grid Integration Council," contact info.

## Default Positions by Topic

**Dynamic rates / V2G export compensation:** Favor dynamic, cost-reflective rates (day-ahead, real-time pricing) over demand charges. Push for export compensation reaching non-NEM, non-QF resources at or near marginal cost. Always ask for **dual participation** — a customer should be able to be on a dynamic rate *and* enrolled in demand response/emergency programs; treat utility objections to dual participation as a barrier to knock down, not settled policy.

**Interconnection (Rule 21 and equivalents):** Interconnection burden should scale to actual grid-safety risk. Oppose requiring full interconnection agreements for one-way (V1G) charging or for isolated/non-grid-parallel backup power systems. Support streamlined, appropriately scoped certification (UL 1741 SA/SB/SC, IEEE 1547); oppose utility-specific add-on requirements (e.g., extra third-party testing) as duplicative. Push for low, cost-justified interconnection fees and dedicated backup-power application pathways separate from standard interconnection portals.

**Managed charging / Automated Load Management (ALM):** Support it, but insist it stay **voluntary and incentive-based** — "a carrot, not a stick." Push for clear technical definitions/standards. Oppose utilities positioning themselves as sole authority over ALM design/evaluation. Favor centralized/orchestrated managed-charging program designs, provided they also fund bidirectional (V2G) hardware incentives, not just one-way load management.

**Demand response (DR) / emergency programs:** Treat EV/V2G as an underused DR resource. Push for telematics-based (not just hardware-submeter) participation and aggregation; push for dual participation with dynamic rates; defend existing EV-specific DR categories (e.g., CA's ELRP Group A.5) against dispatch/compensation cuts or being folded into generic categories. Generally defend and modestly expand existing DR structures rather than seek redesign.

**Submetering / EVSE telematics standards:** Support submetering *and* telematics as parallel, complementary verification pathways — telematics should not be excluded just because hardware submeters exist. Push for clear technical standards, but resist bespoke state-specific requirements when national standardization efforts are already underway; sequence submetering-standard adoption after EV-specific rate designs are authorized, not before. Willing to diverge from allied intervenors (e.g., environmental groups) on the pace/rigor of accuracy standards.

**Backup power / V2H:** Consistently supportive as a resiliency and public-safety benefit (e.g., wildfire-related outage use cases), not just a customer economic feature. Support keeping isolated, non-grid-parallel backup power systems out of full interconnection review. Push utilities to build dedicated, simple backup-power information/notification portals.

**Transportation electrification (TE) funding:** Consistent advocate for sustained/expanded funding — rebate programs, LCFS holdback funds for V2G/V2B and resiliency, CEC programs like Demand Side Grid Support and Distributed Electricity Backup Assets. Oppose pausing or cutting funding programs; escalate to direct legislative coalition letters when program funding is threatened, not just administrative-record comments.

**IRP / grid planning:** Push Integrated Resource Planning and distribution planning to model VGI/V2G accurately rather than treating EVs as pure load — request explicit V1G/V1G+V2G modeling scenarios and higher-V2G sensitivity cases. Push for electric school buses to be recognized as a strong V2G resource (predictable dwell times align with system peaks). Support longer distribution-planning horizons and better incorporation of known EV load into grid-needs assessments.

## Who to Align With / Push Back On

- **Natural allies** (co-file or cite favorably): clean-energy and EV-industry groups (Advanced Energy Economy, CESA/CALSSA, NRDC, EDF, IREC), EV OEM and VGI-tech member companies (Honda, BMW, GM, Nissan, Ford, Stellantis, Tesla, Nuvve, Fermata, Mobility House, WeaveGrid, Wallbox, Enphase, ChargePoint, Enel X, Siemens), and DR/DER aggregators (Leap, Voltus, CPower, Renew Home, Sunrun) — but alignment isn't automatic; VGIC will diverge from allies on specific technical points when warranted.
- **Primary friction points:** California's three large IOUs (PG&E, SCE, SDG&E) on implementation specifics — interconnection-agreement scope, ALM authority, export-compensation completeness, DR compensation levels — while still supporting specific utility proposals VGIC views as beneficial. Keep utility criticism narrow and issue-specific, never categorical.
- **Regulators/staff:** Default to collaborative and appreciative, even while disagreeing with a specific proposal — commend the effort, then ask for refinement.
