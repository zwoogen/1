RE: Clarification of VGIC’s Recommendations on Technical Standards and
Certifications of Bidirectional Charging Systems for the Energy Storage
Solutions Program
VGIC seeks to clarify its recommendations regarding the technical standards and
certification requirements used to determine eligibility for bidirectional charging systems
under the Energy Storage Solutions (ESS) program. As bidirectional charging solutions
continue to evolve, program eligibility criteria must reflect both established safety
standards and the current state of commercially available solutions, without inadvertently
excluding viable commercially available systems that can meaningfully support the grid.
Recommendation #1: Separate Requirements for DC and AC Bidirectional Charging
Systems
Technical standards and certification requirements should be established separately for
DC and AC bidirectional charging systems. This distinction is consistent with existing
interconnection frameworks, which recognize the fundamental differences in how these
distinct bidirectional charging architectures are certified and deployed. Aligning ESS
program eligibility requirements with this structure will help ensure clarity and reduce
ambiguity for participants, program administrators, and technology providers.
Recommendation #2: Align ESS Program Requirements with Distribution System
Interconnection Requirements
To further bolster clarity and reduce ambiguity, the standards and required certifications
for bidirectional charging system ESS program eligibility should be mapped to the
requirements for safe and reliable distribution system interconnection. Requirements for
1

ESS program eligibility that do not also appear in the relevant interconnection
requirements should be identified with a clear explanation as to why the standard or
certification is required.
Recommendation #3: Provide a Grace Period for Equipment That Does Not Meet
Program Communication Protocols Eligibility Requirements
VGIC appreciates the importance of and supports advancing toward a robust and
interoperable bidirectional charging ecosystem, including through the use of open
standards, which may include ISO 15118-20 Amendment 1 for Bidirectional Power Transfer
(BPT) and the Open Charge Point Protocol (OCPP), Version 2.1. These standards are likely
to play an important role over the long term in enabling greater interoperability across
vehicles, charging equipment, and charge station management platforms.
At the same time, the market for bidirectional charging remains in a nascent stage of
development. Many commercially available systems today rely on proprietary or
alternative communication approaches and would not meet strict compliance
requirements if imposed today. Establishing near-term eligibility requirements tied to
these protocols risks excluding viable products currently in the market, thereby limiting
participation, slowing the pace of early deployment and learning, and restricting the
magnitude of system peak load reduction and, in turn, the benefits that can accrue to all
ratepayers.
Connecticut is taking a leadership role as one of the first jurisdictions to move from one-off
demonstrations toward full integration of bidirectional charging systems into a customer
program. As such, program design must reflect current market realities while allowing the
industry a chance to mature. VGIC recommends establishing a defined grace period during
which bidirectional charging systems that are not yet designed to open standards remain
eligible for program participation. This approach would enable continued market
development while providing a clear signal that interoperability standards will become
increasingly important over time.
A time-bound grace period, paired with a commitment to revisit communication protocol
requirements in future program years, strikes an appropriate balance between supporting
market transformation and unlocking customer benefits (both for participating customers
and non-participating ratepayers) without compromising long-term interoperability goals.
This phased approach will help ensure that early market momentum is not disrupted while
positioning the program to transition toward standardized, interoperable solutions as

design and certification frameworks (e.g., IEA Task 53), testing infrastructure (e.g., CharIn
Testivals and California’s new Capital Charge Yard), and commercially available products

Recommendation #4: Coordinate the Consideration of Technical Requirements with
that of Incentive Structure
As a general note, VGIC recognizes that any program design elements that provide
equipment rebates should be balanced to avoid deploying assets that may later become
stranded. However, it is unclear at this stage of the Bidirectional EV Working Group
whether the resulting report and recommendations to PURA will advise that equipment
rebates be provided. VGIC posits that if equipment rebates are not provided, the concern
over stranded assets would be eliminated, as program funds would not be placed at risk in
the first place.
Thank you for considering these recommendations. VGIC would be happy to meet to
discuss or clarify any of the above recommendations at your convenience.
