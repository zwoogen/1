In accordance with the Rule 11.1 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California
Public Utilities Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”)
hereby submits this response to the Motion of Enphase Energy, Inc. to Amend Scoping Ruling and
Schedule to Address Optional Flexible Connection Agreements (“FCA”) and the Underwriters’
Laboratory (“UL”) 3141 Standard for Power Control Systems (“PCS”) in Investor-owned
Utilities’ (“IOU”) Energization Rules (“Motion”) filed by Enphase Energy on September 11,
2024.
I. INTRODUCTION.
It is widely acknowledged that meeting California’s climate goals will require massive
electrification and that the electric grid has not yet been built to accommodate this extra load. The
Commission is working to support the build-out of additional distribution capacity and bring
additional energy resources online. However, bringing all these resources online will take
significant time. The Phase 1 decision in this proceeding has set energization timelines for the
investor-owned utilities (“IOUs”) to meet, but the maximum upstream capacity timelines set by
2

the decision are still incredibly long, with the timeline for a new/upgraded circuit being almost two
years.1 Given the length of these timelines, it is becoming increasingly clear that interim measures
are needed to allow for electrification ahead of grid upgrades.
VGIC has advocated before the Commission since 2020 for leveraging flexible service
connection technologies, including Power Control Systems (“PCS”), alternatively referred to by
the Commission as Automated Load Management (“ALM”) in R.18-12-006, as essential tools for
enabling customers to energize their sites in the face of delays. The Smart Inverter
Operationalization Working Group in the High DER Proceeding (R.21-06-017) has identified
increasing operational flexibility of distributed energy resources (“DERs”) and flexible loads on
the distribution system as a high priority for California.2 This includes the development of flexible
service connections and Limited Load Profiles to enable additional load to come online and be
served cost-effectively. However, policy and rule changes are needed to enable these flexible
service connections.
Therefore, VGIC supports Enphase’s Motion and agrees that the Commission should
leverage Flexible Connection Agreements (“FCA”) enabled by PCS/ALM. Importantly, we
believe that the issues outlined by Enphase must be scoped into a Commission proceeding
immediately. As discussed by VGIC previously, the “Commission should seize the present
opportunity to expeditiously adopt the necessary elements of a flexible service connection
framework.”3 In the four years that VGIC has witnessed this issue be passed between several
proceedings, including the DRIVE OIR (R.18-12-006), High DER OIR (R.21-06-017), TE OIR

II. VGIC SUPPORTS ENPHASE’S MOTION TO INCLUDE POWER CONTROL
SYSTEMS AND FLEXIBLE CONNECTION AGREEMENTS IN TRACK 2 OF
THIS PROCEEDING.
Enphase’s motion requests the modification of Phase 2 Issue 3 to add explicit considerations for
flexible connection agreements (“FCAs”) and Limited Load Profiles (“LLPs”), asking if the
utilities should amend their energization rules to allow customers to utilize flexible connection
agreements that use UL 3141-certified power control systems to implement a Limited Load
Profile. VGIC generally supports this modification to the scoping question, as it is important to
explicitly consider energization rule modifications to enable the use of FCAs. However, we
recommend that the Commission remove the specific reference to UL 3141 in the final scoping
question, as it is premature to limit the PCS certification pathways currently. VGIC suggests the
following language for Phase 2 Issue 3:
4

“Should additional actions beyond compliance with SB 410 and AB 50 be implemented to
improve energization timelines, processes, or tariffs in Phase 2 of this proceeding, including
ordering the utilities to amend their energization rules to allow customers to elect optional flexible
connection agreements that use certified power control systems to implement a Limited Load
Profile that avoids distribution upgrades, thus enabling rapid and cost effective project deployment
and operations?” Currently, Pacific Gas and Electric (“PG&E”) and Southern California Edison
(“SCE”) are conducting pilots for different types of flexible service connections, and VGIC is glad
that the Phase 1 decision will require these utilities to report on their use of these pilots to quickly
energize customers.4 However, the technology for widespread use is ready to be deployed in
California. Enphase highlights that utilizing Power Control Systems (“PCS”) “is the industry
standard for DER load management.”5 PCS systems have already been incorporated into Rule 21
to allow for export controls, and this use case has seen widespread adoption.6 While the UL 3141
standard to enable the full use of PCS for limiting imports is expected to be adopted next month,
the Commission need not wait for this to begin enabling widespread flexible service connections.
Expanding the scoping question to remove the specific reference to UL 3141 will also allow
stakeholders to consider whether other certification pathways could be used to enable the
development of LLPs.
VGIC believes the current barrier to deployment is a lack of clear regulatory focus on the
topic. As discussed by the SIOWG Report, “The CPUC will need to initiate the development of
one or more proceedings for firm and non-firm import limiting requirements, including

III. DEVELOPMENT OF FLEXIBLE SERVICE CONNECTIONS SHOULD BE
TAKEN UP EXPEDITIOUSLY, NOT CONTINUOUSLY REFERRED ACROSS
DIFFERENT REGULATORY VENUES.
VGIC continues to emphasize that the Commission should take up the development of
standardized flexible service connections now. Enphase has submitted this motion to the instant
proceeding after submitting a similar motion in the High DER proceeding (R.21-06-017) and
receiving feedback that “Issues related to energization rules should be addressed in the
energization proceeding.”8 Based on its interpretation of the Commission’s response to Enphase’s
similar motion in the High DER proceeding, VGIC believes that discussing rule modification in
this Energization Timelines proceeding is appropriate. Flexible service connections are a valuable
tool for improving energization timelines, which is an important goal of Phase 2 of this proceeding.

Importantly, the Commission must not further postpone addressing these issues by again
stating that they must be taken up in a different regulatory venue. It is likely that the full
development and implementation of different flexible capacity agreements, especially those that
operate dynamically, will involve work and findings in both the High DER Proceeding and this
instant proceeding. As stated by Enphase, when the Commission discusses energization rule
modification, it will be important to incorporate items from the record of R.21-06-017 Track 3,
including the SIOWG report.9 That record can then be used to develop the actual language for
modifications to Rule 2, 15, 16, and 29/45. Ultimately, energization rule modifications will allow
for the widespread adoption and use of flexible service connections.
VGIC fears the Commission is “missing the forest for the trees,” as it appears to seek
procedural purity rather than addressing a critical, time-sensitive issue that remains one of the only
tools available to address the affordability crisis. VGIC strongly recommends that the Commission
take timely action on this issue.
