In accordance with the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”) hereby submits
these reply comments on the Proposed Decision Adopting Improvements to Distribution Planning
and Project Execution Process, Distribution Resource Planning Data Portals, and Integration
Capacity Analysis Maps (“PD”) issued by Commissioner Houck on September 13, 2024.
I. INTRODUCTION.
As expressed in Opening Comments by VGIC and other parties, California must take
concrete steps to improve distribution planning for new load growth, and the PD takes many
important steps towards achieving this goal. At the same time, the major changes identified in the
PD will require years of implementation, and the additional distribution investments will lead to
large increases in cost. Therefore, the Commission should seek to expand solutions in the near-
term to add additional off-peak load and should seek to unlock optimal amounts of load flexibility
in the longer-term, especially from electric vehicles (“EV”). With these goals in mind, VGIC offers
the following replies to party comments:
1

● VGIC agrees with other parties that the Commission should create widespread flexible
service connection offerings.
● VGIC agrees with other parties recommending that stakeholder feedback should be taken
on the scope of the Part 2 Electrification Impact Study and Load Flexibility Assessment.
II. THE COMMISSION SHOULD CREATE WIDESPREAD FLEXIBLE SERVICE
CONNECTION OFFERINGS.
Multiple parties, including Advanced Energy United (“United”), California Communicty
Choice Association (“CalCCA”), CALSTART, Environmental Defense Fund (“EDF”), and
Natural Resources Defense Council (“NRDC”), commented on the importance of flexible service
connections in allowing customers to get energized more quickly and at a lower cost and noted
that the Commission should take broader action on this issue to scale flexible service connections.1
EDF and NRDC note that “Limiting solutions like flexible service connections to be pilots or one-
off narrow programs will inevitably lead to lower uptake and fewer benefits for individual
customers and ratepayers as a whole.”2 Parties also highlight that while flexible service
connections are beneficial bridging solutions for temporary energization, they can also be scaled
to provide permanent operating parameters to reduce costs for customers and ratepayers.3 VGIC
agrees that these solutions are scalable for bridging and long-term uses and we agree with parties
that Finding of Fact #14, stating that scalable solutions for evaluation do not exist, should be
deleted or modified to acknowledge that scalable solutions can be developed in the future.4 The

VGIC acknowledges that many elements need clarification to scale broader flexible service
connections. As highlighted by CALSTART, scaling flexible service connections will require rules
around the energization study analysis, contractual obligations for the utility or customer, potential

standards, and the tariff modifications required to implement all of these provisions.5 The motion
by Enphase referenced by VGIC in our opening comments also discusses the development of more
standardized Limited Load Profiles that would allow for firm import limit schedules and additional
dynamic, non-firm imports.6 However, the Enphase motion makes clear that the Commission will
need to take action to develop these options.7
The Commission could evaluate a variety of scalable flexible service connection solutions
if a procedural venue is opened for their development and discussion. United and CALSTART
recommend that the Commission adopt a recommendation made by Cal Advocates for the utilities
to file a Tier 2 Advice Letter on their bridging strategies and to follow-up during the annual DPAG
meetings.8 Enphase’s motions have proposed to add scoping questions around flexible service
connections to existing regulatory proceedings, which would allow the Commission to develop a
record to evaluate scalable solutions. VGIC is open to addressing these issues through either a

III. STAKEHOLDER FEEDBACK SHOULD BE TAKEN THE SCOPE OF THE PART
2 ELECTRIFICATION IMPACT STUDY AND LOAD FLEXIBILITY
ASSESSMENT.
The Commission should clarify the scope, roles, and timelines for the EIS Part 2 Study and
associated Load Flexibility Assessment. In Opening Comments, multiple parties commented on
the confusion on the full scope of the study and the roles and responsibilities of different entities
such as the utilities, Kevala, and the California Energy Commission (“CEC”). The PD references
the “Electrification Impact Study Part 2 currently being conducted by Utilities, [emphasis added]”9
and states that the study “is on track to begin in the near term,”10 implying that the scope of the
study has already been decided. However, San Diego Gas and Electric (“SDG&E”) states that “the
scope and timeline for the Utilities’ EIS Part 2 studies are still being developed.”11 United and
Utility Consumers’ Action Network (“UCAN”) highlight that the Commission has never formally
indicated that the utilities would be developing the Part 2 study and reference previous
Commission Rulings and the EIS Part 1 study that suggest that Kevala would be conducting the
Part 2 study.12 SDG&E also highlights that the CEC is creating a Demand Flexibility (DF)
Resource Potential tool that could be incorporated into the EIS Part 2 study, Load Flexibility Study,
or other future studies.13 This has led to uncertainty as to what will be included in the Part 2 study

The Commission should also take additional stakeholder feedback on the scope of the Part
2 study. For example, in Opening Comments, VGIC recommended that the Part 2 Study include
different electric vehicle managed charging scenarios (i.e., including a low, medium, and high
V1G scenario, as well as a V2X bidirectional charging scenario) and to consider the potential for
software- and hardware-based solutions to manage EV charging load specifically for local grid
constraints. However, the PD does not propose to allow for any additional stakeholder feedback
on the Part 2 Study until it is already complete. The EIS Part 1 Study allowed for significant
stakeholder engagement to develop the scope and process for that study, which led to the study
providing more important insights on bottom-up forecasting, needed distribution upgrades, and
cost insights. This stakeholder engagement should be done for the Part 2 study as well. As such,
VGIC supports United’s recommendation that the PD “be revised to align with the Staff Proposal
and allow for stakeholder comment on the EIS Part 2 and Flexible Load DPP Assessment.”14
