In accordance with the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”) hereby submits
these reply comments on the Administrative Law Judge’s Ruling Seeking Comments on Demand
Response Staff Proposal on Bridge Year Funding And Modifying The Schedule For The Bridge
Year Funding Issue (“Ruling”), issued by Administrative Law Judge (“ALJ”) Brandon T. Gerstle
on March 10, 2026, and the Administrative Law Judge’s Ruling Modifying Schedule on Bridge
Year Funding Issue and Posing Supplemental Questions issued by ALJ Brandon T. Gerstle on
April 8, 2026, which extended the comment deadline.
I. INTRODUCTION.
VGIC appreciates the opportunity to respond to party proposals for Demand Response
(“DR”) Bridge Funding for 2028-2029. These comments focus on the critical reliability value
provided by the Emergency Load Reduction Program (“ELRP”), especially as California faces the
likely sunset of other contingency reliability programs and the continued absence of durable
market pathways for distributed energy resources (“DERs”), including bidirectional electric
vehicle charging systems. ELRP remains one of the only programs capable of deploying and
1

scaling bidirectional charging systems at a meaningful scale while also supporting grid reliability
during emergency conditions.
ELRP does remain a pilot and should remain such during the 2028-2029 bridge years. In
subsequent phases of this proceeding and during the next full DR application cycle, the
Commission and stakeholders should consider how to transition ELRP from a pilot into a program
or market-integrated pathway. Generally, VGIC believes that the Commission should come up
with a comprehensive plan for making everyday use of California’s millions of DERs to support
the grid on an ongoing basis in this proceeding.
However, as California transitions toward longer-term market integration for DERs, bridge
funding and program continuity are necessary to avoid disrupting emerging vehicle-grid
integration (“VGI”) investments and reliability capabilities. In response to party opening
comments, VGIC provides the following replies:
• ELRP provides critical reliability value.
• DR bridge years 2028-2029 should be considered as part of the 2023-2027 DR Application
cycle.
• ELRP Group A.5 compensation levels and minimum dispatch hours should remain as-is.
VGIC has also attached its completed inputs for the spreadsheet shared by PG&E for the Joint
Case Management Statement to these comments.
II. ELRP PROVIDES CRITICAL RELIABILITY VALUE.
ELRP should be extended as a critical grid reliability program, unique in its ability to add
capacity during grid emergencies and help prevent them. California is at risk of losing several
major contingency reliability resources over the next several years, including the potential sunset
of the Demand Side Grid Support (“DSGS”) program and the sunset of once-through-cooling
2

plants in California’s Strategic Reliability Reserve.1 Without ELRP, there will be few remaining
pathways for clean DERs, particularly bidirectional charging systems, to continue providing
reliability value in this manner.
The investor-owned utilities (“IOUs”) have already recognized the value of ELRP. Pacific
Gas and Electric (“PG&E”) notes that “ELRP serves an important role as an emergency reliability
tool during periods of extreme system grid stress.” 2 Southern California Edison (“SCE”) similarly
outlines that ELRP “has consistently produced statistically significant load impacts since the pilot's
launch in 2021.” 3 San Diego Gas and Electric (“SDG&E”) agrees and “recognizes that ELRP
Groups A.4 and A.5 provide unique, verified, and dispatchable emergency load relief.” 4 This value
has been particularly significant for SDG&E customers because SDG&E currently does not have
any other residential demand response (“DR”) programs available to EV customers, including
customers with managed charging and/or bidirectional charging systems.
The Public Advocates Office at the California Public Utilities Commission (“Cal
Advocates”) recommends that ELRP not be extended in its current form and instead argue that the
program should either have its funding reduced or even sunset entirely.5 TURN states that funding
should be reduced because the utilities have not spent their full ELRP budgets from 2023 to now.6
However, this perspective ignores that ELRP is a pay-for-performance pilot and costs are only
incurred when participants provide actual load reduction. The relative lack of spending during

III. DR BRIDGE YEARS 2028-2029 SHOULD BE CONSIDERED AS PART OF THE
2023-2027 DR APPLICATION CYCLE.
In Opening Comments, SCE recommends that “2028-2029 DR bridge funding should be
clarified as a continuation of the 2023-2027 program and funding cycle so that unspent 2023-2027
cycle funding can be used to support DR programs and ELRP during the 2028-2029 bridge
period.”19 VGIC strongly agrees that this is the most prudent path forward. Because the Staff
Proposal and bridge funding structure largely extend existing programs with relatively minor
modifications, it is reasonable to consider the bridge period as part of the existing DR funding
cycle.
Allowing rollover of unspent funds from the 2023-2027 cycle will also help preserve
program continuity during a period of significant uncertainty and transition. As discussed by SCE,
allowing for funding rollovers from the 2023-2027 cycle will ensure “continued financial health
of program operations” due to inflation. 20 Rollover funding is also important because ELRP
participation is likely to increase substantially during the bridge years, particularly if DSGS
sunsets.
It remains uncertain how many resources, including battery fleets and aggregated
bidirectional charging systems, may transition from DSGS into ELRP and how this increased
participation will impact available program funding. Allowing rollover funding will increase the

IV. ELRP GROUP A.5 COMPENSATION LEVELS AND MINIMUM DISPATCH
HOURS SHOULD REMAIN AS-IS.
In Opening Comments, some parties recommend modifying the structure of ELRP by
either decreasing the minimum dispatch hours for Groups A.4 and A.5 or reducing ELRP
compensation levels from $2/kWh to $1/kWh.22 Neither of these proposed changes should be
applied to Group A.5, VGI aggregations, at this time. The current compensation structure and
minimum dispatch hours remain necessary to support customer participation, preserve reliability
value, and continue scaling bidirectional charging resources. As detailed below, these proposed
modifications directly work against the program’s overall goals related to grid reliability, the
market transition goals specific to customer group A.5, and the broader objectives of Senate Bill
(“SB”) 676 and Decision (“D.”) 20-12-029.

This reasoning remains directly relevant today, particularly for bidirectional charging customers.
ELRP remains the only major program currently available to deploy and scale bidirectional
charging systems across California. As discussed by VGIC in opening comments, there are no
other DR programs that meaningfully allow for bidirectional charging system participation and
many existing export rate structures, including legacy Net Energy Metering (“NEM”), Net Billing

Without these program structures, customers will be less likely to participate in ELRP and
enrollment in the program could even decrease over time. If ELRP is extended with compensation
that does not enable customer participation, then these resources will be left stranded and unable
to provide reliability value. Additionally, the utilities are likely to continue spending their full

B. Maintaining Dispatch Hours Will Improve Reliability and Policy Learning.
Instead of reducing minimum dispatch hours, the Commission should expand ELRP
dispatch opportunities beyond solely balancing authority EEAs as suggested by parties.26
Expanding event triggers would allow ELRP resources to help prevent system emergencies before
they occur while also unlocking additional reliability and operational value from these resources.
As detailed in Section II above, VGIC agrees with PG&E that utilities should retain discretion to
dispatch the ELRP fleet to address other grid needs, including local distribution constraints and
localized system stress events. Expanding dispatch opportunities while maintaining or even
increasing the 30-hour minimum dispatch requirement would also significantly improve the
amount of operational data available to IOUs, the Commission, and other stakeholders from
bidirectional charging systems, which, to date, has represented a gap in understanding of resource
availability and customer behavior.
With at least 30 hours of annual dispatch data from bidirectional charging systems,
California will be able to develop a much more meaningful understanding of how these systems
respond to DR signals and perform under varying grid conditions. This operational experience is
critical because ELRP is fundamentally a pilot program intended to test new approaches for
deploying bidirectional charging systems and other emerging DER technologies, and is currently

the only pilot available across all three IOU territories that assesses grid services provided
by bidirectional charging systems. The results of ELRP will help inform future permanent
programs and/or market integration pathways for these resources.
Without minimum dispatch hours, ELRP may fail to generate sufficient operational
experience and data to meaningfully inform future policy and market design for bidirectional
charging systems. The Commission cannot risk leaving the 18.5 GW of bidirectional charging
system capacity that the California Energy Commission, with support from the National
Laboratory of the Rockies, finds could be tapped into, as vehicle sit idle 95% of the time,
completely untapped for reliability and critical learning purposes.27 Maintaining the current
dispatch structure will therefore maximize both the near-term reliability value and long-term
policy value provided by these resources.
