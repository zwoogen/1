In accordance with the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”) hereby submits
these reply comments on the Administrative Law Judge’s Ruling Requesting Comment on the Joint
Investor-Owned Utilities Data Assessments Pursuant to Decision 22-11-040 (“Ruling”), issued by
Administrative Law Judge (“ALJ”) on December 27, 2023.
I. INTRODUCTION
VGIC commends the Commission for issuing the ruling seeking comments from parties on
the Joint Investor-Owned Utilities’ (“IOUs”) Data Assessment proposal.1 Decision (“D”) 22-11-
040 adopted a Data Assessment structure that would capture all of the Joint IOUs’ transportation
electrification (“TE”) data. In D.22-11-040, the Commission also determined that the objective of
the Data Assessment is to streamline existing reporting requirements, minimize unnecessary or

II. VGIC OPPOSES IOUS' PROPOSAL TO "MOVE [VGI REPORTING] AWAY
FROM NUMEROUS, DETAILED QUESTIONS TOWARDS FEWER, BROADER
QUESTIONS," AS IT STRAYS TOO FAR FROM D.20-12-029 AND SUBSEQUENT
DECISIONS.
VGIC opposes the IOUs' proposed recommendation related to the VGI Report to “remove
redundant and unnecessary information by moving away from numerous, detailed questions
towards fewer, broader questions.”3 The intent of D.20-12-029 is to collect data on IOUs' VGI
initiatives and the resulting "amount" of VGI, which could include specific measurements on
customer enrollment, megawatt load reduction, deployment of automated load management
(“ALM”)/vehicle-to-everything (“V2X”) technologies, and other quantitative answers to "detailed

III. VGIC OPPOSES ELIMINATING THE “VGI STOCKTAKE,” AS IT IS A
VALUABLE TOOL TO INFORM VGI INITIATIVE/PORTFOLIO
DEVELOPMENT.
The annual VGI Stocktake update required by D.20-12-029 is intended to promote general
awareness of VGI programs, rates, and pilots in order to inform development of each IOUs’ VGI
initiatives. Specifically, the VGI Stocktake helps to ensure utility pilots are not duplicative of
existing pilots or “re-piloting” technology that has already been proven, including in commercial
settings. On the other hand, the VGI Stocktake serves as a repository of information that can reveal
gaps in programs or pilots, thereby providing a key input into the ideation of new VGI programs,

IV. VGIC GENERALLY SUPPORTS CAL ADVOCATES’ RECOMMENDATION TO
REQUIRE TE QUARTERLY REPORTS BE SENT TO ALL TE PROCEEDINGS
SERVICE LISTS AND PRESENT THE DATA AT QUARTERLY PAC
MEETINGS.
In opening comments, Cal Advocates recommends the Commission require the IOUs to
send a public version of their TE quarterly reports to all TE proceedings service lists.5 Furthermore,
Cal Advocates recommends that “each of the IOUs present the data in the public version of the TE
quarterly reports…at the current quarterly [PAC] meetings.”6 Cal Advocates further recommends
that “the TE quarterly reports include, for each program, data on how much money has been spent
to date since the program started and how much funding remains unspent to date.”
VGIC supports Cal Advocates’ recommendations to require the IOUs to send TE quarterly
reports to all TE proceedings service lists and present the data at future IOU quarterly PAC
meetings. VGIC believes Cal Advocates' recommendations align with the Data Collection and
Reporting sections of D.20-12-0297 and D.22-11-040, which deemed the structure “a reasonable
methodology to inventory all IOU TE data and ensure funds are properly spent.”8
