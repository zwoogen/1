1 I. The EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot is Necessary and Appropriate
2 Q: In Opening Testimony, SCE states the “EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot would not provide system relief in
3 2022 in light of limited/no MW potential and is unnecessary in light of other participation opportunities
4 currently open to EV resources.” 1 Do you agree?
5 A. No. SCE’s testimony related to the EV/VGI aggregation pilot is erroneous in several respects. First, it
6 assumes that the EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot is designed solely to target bidirectional EV/EVSE in its service
7 territory. SCE cites the numbers of bidirectional charging stations that are installed, have pending
8 interconnection applications, and expressed interest in Emergency Load Reduction Program (ELRP) so far, but
9 offers no data on the number of EVs in SCE’s service territory, let alone the number of potential aggregators
10 that offer unidirectional “V1G” services in its service territory.2 While VGIC supports the inclusion of
11 bidirectional resources in the EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot, we note this is not the distinguishing feature of the
12 EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot. The distinguishing feature of the EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot is the development of a
13 reliable and accessible pathway for aggregators to leverage EVs, whether through unidirectional or bidirectional
14 configurations. The staff proposal clearly states its focus on aggregators that use “networks of V1G or bi-
15 directionally capable charging stations,” 3 (emphasis added). VGIC believes SCE’s testimony regarding the
16 EV/VGI aggregation pilot may be based on a misinterpretation of the staff concept, and consideration of SCE’s
17 opposition may distract from the critical opportunity to leverage EVs for reliability through this pilot. As noted
18 in opening testimony, VGIC’s analysis shows up to 247 MW of net peak load reduction could be achieved from
19 V1G alone, assuming a modest 5% participation rate.
20 Q: Regardless of the significant potential for V1G, do SCE’s claims about V2G charging compensation
21 have any relevance in the context of ELRP?

21 allowing for the virtual pairing of separately metered EV load with other customer load. Additionally, even for

10 Q. Do you agree with CALSSA’s assertion that the same rules should apply for EV batteries as stationary
11 storage, “as the two resources employ fundamentally the same technology and stationary storage is more
12 well prepared to deliver strong performance in the short term.”8
13 A. Not exactly. While VGIC believes that stationary and mobile storage are technically similar under certain
14 circumstances, there are practical realities that make these resources fundamentally different in the current
15 market. A few of these differences are summarized below:
16  EV owners use their batteries for a different primary use case (i.e., mobility), that is unrelated
17 to managing electricity consumption.
18  VGI-enabled EVs do not receive market transformation support through Self Generation
19 Incentive Program (SGIP), net energy metering (NEM), or other existing pathways that have
20 been available for stationary storage customers and/or providers.
21  EVs can be load-only or bidirectional, whereas stationary storage is, by definition,
22 bidirectional (even if bidirectional means on-site consumption).

10 II. The EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot 30 Hour Dispatch Structure is Appropriate to Support Market
11 Transformation
12 Q. How did the IOUs react to the proposed 30-hour minimum dispatch requirement in the EV/VGI
13 Aggregation Pilot?
14 A. In Opening Testimony, PG&E states that it does not support the 30 hour per season dispatch carveout for
15 VGI resources.9 PG&E states that “mandating IOUs to force dispatch for at least 30 hours without an
16 emergency does not seem to align with how and why ELRP was developed.”10
17 Q. Do you agree with this assessment?
18 A. No. I believe that the 30-hour minimum is consistent with the intent of the original ELRP program design.
19 While it is certainly possible that the 30-hour minimum could lead to dispatch hours outside of a true
20 “emergency” I believe this is still appropriate for a few reasons. First, having a more consistent and predictable
21 dispatch schedule will help to acclimate EV customers and aggregators to the overall ELRP regime. While a
22 more limited number of hours could be contemplated, this would run a greater risk of failing to deliver MW

2 and may opt-out during the critical times. In essence there is significant value in the ability to “train” customers
3 and aggregators on how the ELRP program functions well in advance of a true emergency. Second, even if
4 dispatch hours occur outside of a true emergency, they can still be targeted to high value time periods. For

8 compensation rate would not necessarily reflect any additional value provided to the distribution system since
9 EVs function as a distributed resource.
10 Q. What does PG&E say regarding ELRP eligibility for customers participating in NEM and SGIP?
11 A. PG&E notes that bidirectional vehicles co-located with solar participating in a NEM tariff cannot also
12 participate in the ELRP program, and that bidirectional vehicles are not eligible to receive incentives under
13 SGIP.11
14 Q. Do you believe this constitutes a rationale to exclude EVs from greater ELRP participation?
15 A. No. If anything, this emphasizes the need for ELRP to encourage participation from EVs since they are
16 largely excluded from other DER incentive programs. For example, VGIC notes that EVSE are also not eligible
17 to receive incentives under the SGIP. The EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot’s 30-hour dispatch requirement will
18 provide aggregators with a reliable value stream that can support finance-ability, product development, and
19 overall market development. As such, VGIC strongly recommends the Commission adopt the 30-hour dispatch
20 requirement in the EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot as it will critically support market transformation for VGI
21 aggregators and customers alike.

4 Q. What other party recommendations were offered related to the EV/VGI Pilot?
5 A. In Opening Testimony, ev.energy details several recommended improvements to the EV/VGI Aggregation
6 Pilot.12 These included the following recommendations:
7 1. The Energy Division should define “aggregators” with sufficient breadth.
8 2. The EV/VGI Aggregation Pilot should not require aggregators to integrate directly with CAISO.
9 3. Program settlement should use baseline methodology informed by counterfactuals rather than
10 historical data.
11 4. Telematics and L1/L2 EVSE data should all be considered equally for the purposes of
12 performance settlement.
13 Q. Does VGIC generally agree with these recommendations?
14 A. Yes. In addition to these recommendations from ev.energy, VGIC also agrees with the Joint DR Parties’
15 recommendation to reduce or eliminate interconnection fees in exchange for bidirectional EVSE purchase and
16 installation.13 This can help overcome barriers to bidirectional charger deployment and level the playing field
17 with stationary storage, which has access to SGIP technology incentives to help offset the upfront costs of
18 system installation and interconnection.
