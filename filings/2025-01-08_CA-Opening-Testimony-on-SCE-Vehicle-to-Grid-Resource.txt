6 II. BACKGROUND ON CURRENT AND NEAR FUTURE POTENTIAL FOR VGI
7 TO SUPPORT RATEPAYER AFFORDABILITY GOALS AS WELL AS GRID

9 Q. SCE’s Amended Rate Design Proposals details that “Despite their considerable
10 energy storage potential, EVs actively engage in transportation services for a
11 fraction of the day and can remain idle for a significant portion of each day. This
12 underutilization of available energy in mobile-battery assets underscores a latent
13 resource that, if tapped effectively, could help address the burgeoning challenges
14 facing the grid as it integrates increasing volumes of renewable energy sources.”
15 Does VGIC agree?
16 A. Yes. VGIC agrees that EVs are underutilized as a battery energy storage resource and
17 represent untapped potential to support the integration of renewable energy. As such,
18 VGI efforts broadly represent a significant opportunity to both advance the VGI industry
19 and deliver much needed grid benefits. There is no doubt that EVs on the road today, plus
20 those that will be by the anticipated by the 2027 launch of VGRP, have the technical
21 capability to meaningfully support the integration of renewable energy resources through
22 a mix of unidirectional (“V1G”) and bidirectional vehicle-to-everything (“V2X”)
23 charging activities. However, market incentives to support this integration of renewable
3

1 energy through opportunities like that proposed in SCE’s VGRP have been slower to
2 develop.
3 Q. SCE’s Amended Rate Design Proposals concludes by stating: “By incentivizing
4 customer participation in V2G, grid-as-a-service exports and providing a
5 transparent compensation mechanism, SCE paves the way for a sustainable and
6 resilient energy future while empowering customers to actively contribute to grid
7 decarbonization efforts and manage their energy bills.” Does VGIC agree?
8 A. Yes. VGIC agrees that in addition to the renewable energy integration benefit detailed
9 above, incentivizing customers to participate in V2G exports supports a resilient energy
10 future in which customers are empowered to contribute to grid decarbonization efforts
11 and manage their energy bills.
12 Q. Can EVs support other important policy goals, including affordability for all
13 ratepayers – not just EV drivers – and increased system reliability?
14 A. Yes. VGI strategies, including V1G managed charging and V2X bidirectional charging, if
15 designed appropriately, can reduce costs to all ratepayers, including EV owners and non-
16 EV customers. By supporting the more efficient use of existing grid infrastructure and
17 accelerating transportation electrification (and the resulting kWh sales), average rates for
18 all customers can be lower than they would be without effective VGI strategies.
19 Furthermore, EVs can meaningfully reduce net peak load through both V1G managed
20 charging and V2X bidirectional charging activities. This is a critical feature of VGI as
21 California faces increased extreme weather events and resulting emergency reliability
22 conditions.

1 Q. Is VGIC confident that EVs can provide a meaningful contribution, in MW terms, if
2 encouraged to do so through appropriate export credits for bidirectional charging
3 customers?
4 A. Yes. VGIC is confident that widespread EV exports can become a reality in response to
5 appropriate export credits for eligible customers, including along the anticipated
6 implementation timeline for SCE’s VGRP (i.e., beginning in 2027). If the proposed
7 VGRP is adopted (with VGIC’s recommended modifications) there are no fundamental
8 technical barriers that would prevent EVs from contributing. However, VGIC’s optimism
9 in this regard is also tempered by the reality that V2G systems have not yet been
10 deployed at such a significant scale and that there are still some unknown factors
11 regarding overall customer acceptance and participation. We are encouraged by SCE’s
12 2023 customer surveys, detailed in its Amended Rate Design Proposals, which provide
13 empirical evidence of customer acceptance and appetite for V2G energy exports,
14 detailing that 81% of surveyed customers expressed at least “a little interested.” Notably,
15 unlike power plants that have a singular function, the primary role of EVs is to support
16 customers’ transportation needs, not grid affordability, reliability, or decarbonization
17 needs. As such, to be successful the VGRP must be designed in a manner that puts the
18 customers’ perspective first and foremost. VGIC believes this can and should be
19 achieved. Additionally, since this is a novel type of grid resource, any incremental
20 participation (even if small) will be beneficial and is still worth pursuing as part of a
21 comprehensive approach to advancing VGI pursuant to Senate Bill 676, which aims to
22 maximize feasible and cost-effective VGI by 2030.

20 certain event triggers. Similar to ELRP, the DSGS Option 3 program is not a
21 sustainable, cost-based offering, and is only available until funding, determined
22 by the legislature and governor, is no longer available. Funding is shared across
23 all options and resources in DSGS.

3 Q. Please explain the concept of path dependence and how it relates to V2G rate design
4 decisions made today.
5 A. “Path dependence” refers to the influence of past decisions on future outcomes, hindering
6 the implementation of alternative solutions. In the case of V2G customers, VGIC is
7 concerned that relying too heavily on a single dynamic rate approach for all customers, a
8 recommendation made in Cal Advocates’ testimony and detailed further in Section V
9 below, would lock in a specific future that may not, in fact, be in the best interest of
10 ratepayers. Notably, Cal Advocates has published findings suggesting that optimized VGI
11 implementation can reduce electrification-driven distribution system costs from the
12 Commission’s estimated $50 billion through 2035 down to $22.7 billion.40 Given the
13 magnitude of these numbers, the stakes are incredibly high, especially for low-income
14 and other vulnerable California ratepayers already feeling an outsized energy cost burden.
15 However, to achieve the reductions modeled by Cal Advocates, IOUs will need to engage
16 EV customers at a significant enough scale for these optimized load shapes to have a
17 beneficial impact on the total required distribution system upgrades. VGIC is concerned
18 that pursuing only dynamic rates, with the only available evidence showing that V2G
19 customers are not interested in participating,41 risks a gamble with path dependence,
20 wherein SCE would be “locked in” to offering only dynamic rates, with no opportunity to

3 Q. Do you believe that further expanding SCE’s Expanded Pilot would yield optimal
4 V2G participation and the resulting grid benefits?
5 A. No. As detailed in Section III above, the ways in which EV drivers use their vehicles are
6 diverse, and the ways in which these vehicles interact with the grid are perhaps even
7 more diverse. To transition the VGI market from early V2G demonstrations and pilots to
8 large-scale deployments that can meaningfully support the grid and provide grid edge
9 energy storage capacity at scale, V2G customers may benefit from access to a menu of
10 export compensation options. EV customers have a diverse set of needs and serve a
11 variety of use cases characterized by various duty cycles, dwell times, vehicle battery
12 sizes, charger configurations, and site load characteristics. As noted above in Section III,
13 the 2019-2020 VGI Working Group defined, sorted, assessed, and ranked 2,500 potential
14 VGI use cases. Meanwhile, SCE’s Charge Ready Transport program, the nation’s largest
15 medium- and heavy-duty make-ready charging program, demonstrates a rich array of
16 various customer segments and charger sizes.
17 Promoting grid-friendly behavior, including V2G exports, from an exceptionally diverse
18 subset of utility customers presents a unique challenge. This use case diversity strongly
19 suggests that no one single compensation mechanism will optimally capture all potential
20 beneficial V2G participation. This is consistent with the argument made by SCE in its
21 VGRP proposal:64 holistic frameworks and strategies consisting of a mix of static retail
