RE: Response of the Vehicle Grid Integration Council to Pacific Gas and Electric Advice
Letter 7378-E-A Supplemental: PG&E’s Request for Modification to Phase II Scope for
PG&E’s Vehicle-to-Everything Pilot #3 (V2X Microgrid Pilot)
Dear Sir or Madam:
Pursuant to the provisions of General Order 96-B, the Vehicle Grid Integration Council
(“VGIC”) hereby submits this response to the above-referenced Advice Letter 7378-E-A of Pacific
Gas and Electric Company (“PG&E”), Supplemental: PG&E’s Request for Modification to Phase
II Scope for PG&E’s Vehicle-to-Everything Pilot #3 (V2X Microgrid Pilot) (“Advice Letter”) on
August 6, 2025.
VGIC appreciates PG&E’s work to advance vehicle-grid integration (“VGI”) in its service
territory by conducting three VGI pilots and providing learnings on how to best integrate electric
vehicles (“EVs”) into the grid and leverage bidirectional charging capabilities. The V2X Microgrid
Pilot discussed in this Advice Letter has yielded valuable learnings but has also seen challenges.
Given this, PG&E is requesting that Phase II of the microgrid pilot transition into a hybrid support
model where PG&E staff continues supporting V2X readiness in a technical consultancy role at
Microgrid Incentive Program sites.
Generally, VGIC supports PG&E’s request to transition away from the original Phase II
scope. However, VGIC recommends the V2X Microgrid Pilot resources be redirected to provide
additional funding and staff support for the other two V2X Pilots: Residential and Commercial.
Moreover, VGIC believes that it is inappropriate for PG&E to publicly disclose specific details on
vendors included in Appendix I of the Advice Letter. The Commission should remind PG&E and
other utilities to file sensitive information confidentially.
I. VGIC SUPPORTS PG&E’S TRANSITION AWAY FROM THE PHASE II V2X
MICROGRID PILOT.
It has long been acknowledged, and discussed previously in VGIC filings, that the V2X
Microgrid Pilot includes the most complex projects of the three VGI pilots approved in Resolution
1

E-5192. While PG&E’s VGI pilots are testing deployment strategies for new bidirectional
charging solutions, including developing customer engagement strategies and modernizing utility
operations and planning, community microgrids are themselves still in a pilot stage and often
utilize nascent equipment and configurations. This has created unique challenges for the V2X
Microgrid Pilot.
In response to these challenges, PG&E is requesting to transition the Phase II pilot to a
hybrid support model. This will allow customers participating in the Microgrid Incentive Program
(MIP) to leverage bidirectional technologies in their community microgrids. VGIC believes that
directing efforts to MIP to integrate bidirectional EVs into community microgrids is reasonable,
given that it is expected that communities building these microgrids will be participating in MIP.
PG&E does not need to duplicate efforts in a separate pilot at this time. On the other hand, MIP
and community microgrid uptake overall has been slow. As such, VGIC believes that the resources
and funding previously directed for use in V2X Microgrid Phase II should be used to cover MIP
and community microgrid support, which is likely to remain minimal, as well as supplementing
existing staff support and funding for the more active and impactful Residential and
Commercial V2X Pilots.
II. PG&E AND OTHER UTILITIES SHOULD FILE DETAILS ON VENDORS
CONFIDENTIALLY.
While VGIC is understanding of PG&E’s request in the Advice Letter, it was inappropriate
for PG&E to publish details on individual vendors in Appendix I in a public filing. Publicly
revealing vendor names was unnecessary to provide the Energy Division staff with sufficient
responses to data requests for useful context on pilot challenges. Publicly attributing specific
technical or contractual difficulties to a single company risks reputational harm while offering
little value to regulatory decision-making.
Typically, public filings such as Advice Letters describe vendor-related issues without
disclosing specific names, which allows the Commission and stakeholders to understand the nature
of the problem without singling out individual companies. Indeed, in the body of this Supplemental
Advice Letter on page 3, PG&E appropriately summarized vendor and contractor challenges in
general terms without naming companies. That level of detail was sufficient and should have been
what was included in the public-facing document.
Moreover, PG&E itself acknowledges that these challenges were not the result of unique
failures by any individual vendor but rather reflected broader issues with the nascency of the
bidirectional charging market. As PG&E states: “If we are to expand this microgrid pilot and add
more EVSE vendors and EV OEMs in different microgrid settings, it is a practical certainty that
there will be similar problems of significant magnitude/impact, and the pilot will continue to
2

experience significant delays.”1 This demonstrates that the problems encountered by PG&E in the
deployment of the V2X Microgrid Pilot were industry-wide, not vendor-specific. Therefore, it was
unnecessary to name individual vendors in the public filing.
VGIC understands that the details in Appendix I were provided in response to Energy
Division questions, but PG&E should have filed that portion of the Advice Letter confidentially.
The Commission has long-standing procedures to allow utilities to provide sensitive information
under seal, ensuring regulators have full access without unnecessarily disclosing commercially
sensitive details to the public. Moreover, utilities have long closely followed the norm of disclosing
sensitive information confidentially, and the departure from that norm in this case is entirely
unexpected and unnecessary.
By instead choosing to single out a vendor in a public filing, PG&E departed from
regulatory norms and created the risk of chilling future participation from innovative companies
who may be less willing to partner with utilities if early-stage challenges are likely to be publicized.
This is particularly concerning given the relatively nascent market for bidirectional charging
solutions and vendors, and the immense pressures already creating challenges for that market,
including the uncertainty about how and when mass-market, cost-based compensation
mechanisms will be made available to customers eager to adopt this technology. For these reasons,
VGIC urges the Commission and Energy Division staff to remind PG&E and other utilities that
vendor-specific information should be submitted confidentially in future Advice Letters.
III. CONCLUSION.
VGIC appreciates the opportunity to submit this response to the Advice Letter and looks
forward to collaborating with the Energy Division and stakeholders on this important initiative.
