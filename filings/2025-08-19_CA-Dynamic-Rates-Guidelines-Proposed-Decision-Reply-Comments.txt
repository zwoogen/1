In accordance with the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”) hereby submits
these reply comments on the Proposed Decision Adopting Guidelines for Pacific Gas and Electric
Company, Southern California Edison Company, and San Diego Gas & Electric Company On
Demand Flexibility Rate Design Proposals (“Proposed Decision” or “PD”), issued by
Administrative Law Judges Rajan Mutialu and Carolyn Sisto on July 25, 2025.
I. INTRODUCTION.
VGIC appreciates the opportunity to reply to party comments on the Demand Flexibility
(“DF”) guidelines. This next stage of DF rates is critical to enabling customers of the investor-
owned utilities (“IOUs”), Pacific Gas and Electric (“PG&E”), Southern California Edison
(“SCE”), and San Diego Gas and Electric (“SDG&E”), to provide valuable load flexibility. VGIC
offers the following responses to party comments.
II. PARTIES AGREE THAT THE COMMISSION SHOULD REQUIRE THE
UTILITIES TO PROVIDE DYNAMIC EXPORT RATES.
1

VGIC agrees with Sierra Club and the Solar Energy Industry Association (“SEIA”) that
the PD is deficient because it fails to require IOUs to include export compensation in their DF rate
proposals. As discussed by both Sierra Club and SEIA, the Commission adopted DF principles
that state that DF rate proposals should include marginal cost-based compensation for exports.
Specifically, SEIA notes that “the PD, in recounting all parties’ positions on this issue, and the
basis therefore, presents no advocacy or justification for the exclusion of export compensation
from the Large IOUs’ demand flexibility rate proposals. A Commission decision must be
supported by its findings. This determination is not” (emphasis added).1 Therefore, the
Commission should require the IOUs to provide export compensation in their DF rates, a finding
that is sufficiently supported by the record of this proceeding.
III. UTILITIES SHOULD BE REQUIRED TO OFFER COMPLIANT DYNAMIC
RATES EVEN IF EXTENSIONS ARE GRANTED.
In Opening Comments, all three IOUs ask for extensions to provide proposals that comply
with the guidance in this decision. VGIC understands that significant work will need to be done to
offer comprehensive DF rates, but the IOUs should be required to offer pilots and rates in the near-
term that allow customers to meaningfully access dynamic rates. In response to the three IOUs’
requests, VGIC believes:
• SDG&E: SDG&E requests an extension to propose dynamic rates in late 2026,2 which
should be denied. SDG&E is currently the only IOU territory without a dynamic import
pilot. In A.21-12-006 approving SDG&E’s dynamic export rate pilot, settling parties
agreed that SDG&E should defer consideration of a dynamic import rate proposal until the

dropping out of demand flexibility by losing access to dynamic rates.

IV. DUAL PARTICIPATION IN DYNAMIC RATES AND OTHER DEMAND
RESPONSE PROGRAMS SHOULD BE CLARIFIED.
VGIC generally agrees with Leap and Voltus that dual participation with Demand
Response (“DR”) should be addressed by the Commission.5 However, VGIC recommends that, at
a minimum, the PD explicitly allow dual participation in dynamic rates and any out-of-market
emergency DR programs, such as the Emergency Load Reduction Program (“ELRP”) and the
Demand Side Grid Support (“DSGS”) Program. This dual participation was permitted in the
expanded dynamic rate pilots and strengthens grid reliability.6 VGIC agrees that dual participation
with supply-side DR programs is more complex and should be taken up in a new regulatory venue,
such as a new DR proceeding as described by Leap and Voltus. 7
V. CALIFORNIA SHOULD CONTINUE TO PURSUE METHODS BEYOND
DYNAMIC RATES TO UNLOCK DEMAND FLEXIBILITY.
VGIC agrees with Sierra Club’s recommendation to not forego other opportunities to
advance rate design that unlocks demand flexibility, including through larger time-of-use peak to
off-peak differentials and Critical Peak Pricing offerings.8 Additionally, diverse demand flexibility
programs, such as DR options and the distribution optimization programs outlined by VGIC in
Opening Comments,9 should continue to be offered to customers. There are a variety of ways to
unlock maximum demand flexibility potential, but “current rate offerings in between tiered rates
and demand flexible rates are lacking.”10 EV customer profiles are diverse, with varying

VI. PROCEDURAL CLARITY IS NEEDED GIVEN PG&E AND SCE’S ONGOING
APPLICATIONS DISCUSSING DYNAMIC RATES.
VGIC is concerned with procedural conflicts for PG&E’s implementation of dynamic rates
posed by this PD. As raised by Small Business Utility Advocates (“SBUA”) in Opening
Comments, PG&E is currently discussing dynamic rates in its General Rate Case (“GRC”) Phase
II. Yesterday, the proceeding schedule for PG&E’s GRC Phase II was suspended due to an
unrelated error in PG&E calculations, but it is unclear how guidance from this decision will be
incorporated into PG&E’s ongoing GRC schedule. The Commission should coordinate to ensure
that the ongoing schedule for PG&E’s GRC Phase II considers needed modifications to PG&E’s
original proposal and that intervenors have sufficient time to prepare intervenor testimony after
PG&E submits updated testimony.
