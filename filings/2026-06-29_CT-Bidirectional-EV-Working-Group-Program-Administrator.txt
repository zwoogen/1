RE: VGIC Comments in Response to Working Group Facilitator’s Request: Proposed
Rollout Plan, Proposed Incentives, and Island Requirement
VGIC commends the Bidirectional EV Working Group for a robust and comprehensive process
to develop a report in response to the Connecticut Public Utilities Regulatory Authority’s
(PURA’s) December 17th, 2025, final decision in Docket No. 25-08-05. In the decision, PURA
created the Bidirectional EV Working Group to develop recommendations for incorporating
bidirectional EVs into Connecticut's Energy Storage Solutions (ESS) Program.
The working group facilitator requested member feedback on the proposed rollout plan,
proposed incentives, and island requirement. VGIC welcomes the opportunity to provide these
comments.
I. Proposed Rollout Plan
Over the past several months, stakeholders engaged in facilitated dialogue to address the five
directives established by PURA: cross-program coordination; technical requirements; program
terms & conditions; incentive structures; and light-duty vs. med/heavy-duty. At the June 12,
2026, meeting of the Working Group, the Program Administrators (PAs) shared initial
recommendations. VGIC welcomes the opportunity to offer our support for the recommendations
with modifications as discussed below.
1. Launch the Residential Program for Bidirectional EVs in 2027
VGIC recommends that PURA direct the PAs to launch residential bidirectional EV participation
in the ESS Program in 2027, rather than delaying residential participation pending additional
pilot results. VGIC recognizes the value of the IES (Innovative Energy Solutions) pilots, the
MassCEC V2X Pilot, and other early deployments in informing future program design. These
efforts provide important information on installation costs, customer participation, technology
1

performance, enrollment processes, and implementation challenges. A durable program will
allow the early momentum from these pilots to support market development.
Automakers are expanding the availability of vehicles that support grid-aware charging and
bidirectional capabilities. Major manufacturers, including Ford, General Motors (Chevrolet,
Cadillac, and GMC), Nissan, Kia, Volvo, and Polestar, are already marketing vehicles with
features that enable managed charging or bidirectional energy flows. Tesla recently launched its
PowerShare Grid Support offering in Texas, marking the first large-scale V2G program from a
major automaker using a lower-cost AC-based architecture. Tesla has also announced plans to
launch the same offering in California in coordination with the state’s three major investor-
owned utilities. In addition, manufacturers including Toyota, Honda and Acura, Rivian, Lucid,
BMW, Volkswagen, and Stellantis have announced plans to introduce bidirectional capabilities
across upcoming vehicle platforms. These announcements suggest that bidirectional functionality
will become widely available across most major light-duty EV platforms within the next two to
three years.
Charging equipment manufacturers are also bringing new bidirectional products to market.
Companies including dcbel, Wallbox, Ford, GM Energy, and Tesla already offer bidirectional
charging systems. Additional manufacturers, including Emporia, ChargePoint, Autel, SolarEdge,
and Enphase, have announced upcoming products designed to support bidirectional operation.
For example, Enphase has announced plans to begin volume production of its IQ Bidirectional
EV Charging Platform in late 2026. Taken together, the industry is primed to deliver
bidirectional charging solutions to residential customers at scale. By launching residential
bidirectional EV participation through the ESS Program, Connecticut will send an important
message to automobile OEMs and charger manufacturers that regulators understand the role
bidirectional charging can play in supporting a lower-cost and more efficient grid.
VGIC recognizes that the Working Group identified several residential implementation issues,
including coordination with managed charging, DERMS integration, enrollment processes,
customer control, vehicle readiness, and the potential for multiple vendors to be involved at a
single site. To address these issues, the PAs could initially limit participation through enrollment
caps, allowing Connecticut to begin building operational experience while managing
administrative complexity and customer experience risks.
Launching a residential program in 2027 would position Connecticut as a national leader in
bidirectional charging policy. It would create a pathway for customers, manufacturers, installers,
aggregators, and utilities to begin scaling residential bidirectional charging in a controlled and
transparent manner. It would also ensure that Connecticut captures early learning from actual
program participation, rather than relying only on pilots or experience from other jurisdictions.

VGIC therefore recommends that PURA direct the PAs to include residential bidirectional EVs
in the ESS Program beginning in 2027, with a phased implementation structure that can be
updated as the market matures.
Approve Proposals 1, 2, & 3 as Presented
2.
VGIC supports approval of Proposals 1, 2, and 3 as presented. These proposals provide a
workable foundation for incorporating bidirectional EVs-EVSE into the ESS Program while
maintaining appropriate safety, technical, and administrative requirements.
VGIC supports Proposal 1, which would enroll the EVSE rather than the EV or the combined
EV-EVSE system in the ESS Program. This approach is administratively practical and consistent
with how distributed energy resource programs typically qualify and manage grid-connected
equipment. The EVSE is the appropriate point of program enrollment because it is the equipment
located at the customer site and connected to the electrical system for interconnection review and
approval.
VGIC also supports Proposal 2, which would establish a Qualified Product List for EVSE rather
than for vehicles. This is the right point of program qualification because the EVSE is the device
that will interconnect with the customer site and meet the necessary safety requirements for
interconnection. However, VGIC encourages the PAs to provide clarity on how they define both
“vehicle-to-grid capable” and “DERMS integration.” The QPL must have explicit and clear
requirements to avoid confusion and promote transparency.
VGIC supports Proposal 3, which would require bidirectional EVSE to follow appropriate UL
1741 certification pathways. DC-connected bidirectional charging systems should be eligible
through UL 1741 SB, while AC-connected systems should be eligible through the forthcoming
UL 1741 SC pathway or other applicable UL 1741 SB CRD (certification requirement decision)
for paired EV/EVSE bidirectional charging system certification pathways. This distinction is
important because DC and AC bidirectional charging systems are designed, certified, and
deployed differently. Aligning ESS eligibility with the applicable interconnection certification
pathways will provide clarity for customers, manufacturers, installers, utilities, and program
administrators.
3. Modify Proposal 5 by Eliminating UL 9741 Requirement
VGIC further supports Proposal 5 with the modification to eliminate the UL 9741 requirement.
VGIC supports the requirements for bidirectional EVSE to meet applicable safety standards,
either UL 2202 or UL 2594, as applicable. However, it was recently brought to our attention that
UL 9741 generally applies to DC-based bidirectional charging systems. Requiring devices to
3

meet UL 9741 could exclude AC bidirectional charging systems that follow the UL 1741 SC +
SAE J3072 approach simply because UL 9741 doesn’t cover that system architecture.
4. Modify Proposal 3 by Delaying ISO 15118-20 Requirement
As discussed in VGIC’s earlier comments on communications standards, VGIC supports the
long-term transition toward open, interoperable protocols, including ISO 15118-20 for
bidirectional power transfer. However, VGIC recommends that PURA modify Proposal 4 by
delaying any mandatory ISO 15118-20 requirement until third-party certification, testing
infrastructure, and commercially available compliant products are sufficiently mature. Many
bidirectional charging systems available today rely on proprietary or alternative communication
approaches while still supporting safe operation, dispatch, telemetry, customer control, and
performance verification. Making ISO 15118-20 a near-term eligibility requirement could
therefore exclude certain commercially available products.
PURA should establish a defined grace period for bidirectional EVSE that meets the ESS
Program’s functional requirements but does not yet demonstrate ISO 15118-20 compliance. This
phased approach would preserve early market momentum while providing a clear signal that the
program will move toward standardized communication protocols over time. It would also
maintain the program’s safety and performance foundations by continuing to require applicable
interconnection, certification, and program-dispatch requirements while avoiding a protocol-
specific gatekeeping requirement before the market is ready.
5. Prepare Early for Solar Customer Participation
PURA should also direct the PAs to prepare early for participation by customers with existing
solar systems. Residential bidirectional EV adoption is likely to overlap significantly with the
same customer segment that has already installed rooftop solar, home batteries, smart panels, or
other distributed energy resources. Early pilots have shown that integrating bidirectional EVs at
these sites can be more complex than enrolling a stand-alone charger. Utilities, vendors, and
installers may need to address interactions among solar inverters, EV chargers, backup power
systems, meters, customer load panels, export limits, netting rules, and program dispatch
requirements. Without early planning, these issues could slow enrollment, create inconsistent
customer experiences, or lead to unnecessary interconnection and billing complications.
VGIC recommends that the PAs use the 2027 launch period to develop clear processes for solar-
paired bidirectional EV systems. This should include standardized intake questions, installer
guidance, metering and telemetry requirements, coordination with existing net metering or
distributed generation tariffs, and clear rules for how bidirectional EV exports will be measured
and compensated when located behind the same meter as solar. Preparing for solar customer
4

participation now will allow Connecticut to manage complexity in a controlled manner while
avoiding the need to redesign the program after customers and vendors are already in the market.
II. Proposed Incentives
VGIC supports the PA’s proposed incentive structure, particularly the decision to rely primarily
on performance-based incentives for bidirectional EV participation in ESS program. This
approach aligns compensation with the actual grid value delivered during dispatch events, and it
is consistent with PURA’s direction to move away from higher upfront incentives toward
Construct 5, which places greater weight on measured performance. Because bidirectional EVs
are transportation assets first and grid resources second, a performance-based structure
appropriately rewards customers and aggregators when vehicles are available, enrolled, and
discharging during system need.
VGIC also agrees that the incentive design should reflect cost-effectiveness considerations and
the current maturity of the bidirectional charging market. The bidirectional EV working group
discussions highlighted the fact that bidirectional EV costs and performance remain uncertain,
particularly around bidirectional EVSE costs, interconnection, installation complexity, and
expected event participation. In this context, performance-based incentives provide a practical
near-term structure that supports commercial deployment while allowing the PA and PURA to
gather data from early ESS program participation and related pilots. VGIC supports continuing
to evaluate whether targeted upfront incentives may be appropriate in future program years,
particularly for residential or priority customer segments, but agrees that the initial structure
should emphasize performance, administrative simplicity, customer experience, and
demonstrated cost-effective grid value.
III. Island Requirement
VGIC recommends that bidirectional EVs and EVSE be allowed, but not required, to provide
home backup power or islanding capability as a condition of ESS Program participation. VGIC
recognizes that resiliency is an important objective of the ESS Program and that islanding
capability can provide meaningful customer value during outages. However, bidirectional EVs
can provide grid value to the ESS Program through controlled export during active events even if
the system does not also provide whole-home or partial-home backup power. Requiring
islanding capability for all bidirectional EV systems would risk adding cost, installation
complexity, and customer friction in ways that could slow early market participation without
improving the system’s ability to deliver performance during ESS dispatch events.
5

VGIC therefore recommends a flexible approach. Customers who want backup power should be
permitted to install bidirectional EV systems with islanding capability. However, this capability
should remain optional. For many customers, the incremental cost and complexity associated
with backup power may exceed what is needed to safely export to the grid under ESS Program
dispatch. Making backup power optional would preserve the resiliency pathway for customers
who value it, while allowing other customers and aggregators to participate with simpler export-
capable systems that meet applicable safety, interconnection, telemetry, and performance
requirements. This approach better aligns program eligibility with the core ESS value stream:
verified discharge during system need.
IV. Conclusion
VGIC appreciates the opportunity to participate in the Bidirectional EV Working Group and to
provide these comments. VGIC supports the overall direction of the recommendations and
commends the PAs for developing a thoughtful framework that recognizes the technical,
administrative, and customer-experience issues associated with bringing bidirectional EV into
the ESS Program.
