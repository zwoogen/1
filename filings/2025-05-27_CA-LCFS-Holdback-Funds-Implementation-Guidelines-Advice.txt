I. INTRODUCTION.
VGIC is a 501(c)6 membership-based advocacy group committed to advancing the role of
electric vehicles (“EV”) and vehicle-grid integration (“VGI”) through policy development,
education, outreach, and research. VGIC supports the transition to a decarbonized transportation
and electric sector by ensuring the value from EV deployments and flexible EV charging and
discharging is recognized and compensated to achieve a more reliable, affordable, and efficient
electric grid.
II. DISCUSSION.
VGIC strongly supports the overall intent of the Joint Advice Letter and streamlining
approvals for the use of LCFS holdback funds. These funds offer a valuable opportunity to support
transportation electrification and advance grid-beneficial EV programs. Critically, VGI initiatives
offer one of the only tools to increase the efficiency of the existing grid and mitigate the need for
new distribution system upgrades, which, in turn, can stave off the mounting pressure on utility
rates. VGIC welcomes the California Air Resources Board’s (“CARB”) updated LCFS regulation,

Holdback funding represents a no-regrets strategy to launch innovative, non-ratepayer-
funded programs that can deliver ratepayer benefits and generate critical data to inform broader VGI
market development to ensure the availability of EVs as DERs in the California’s long-term energy
planning toolkit.
A. VGIC Strongly Supports CPUC Directing the IOUs to Comply with the Latest CARB
Regulation and Guidance to Minimize Misalignment.
As detailed in the Advice Letter, substantial changes to the LCFS regulation were made in
the 2024 Amendments adopted by CARB. The IOUs find themselves in a unique situation, required
to comply with both CARB’s guidance in the LCFS regulation and CPUC’s guidance in Decision
(“D.”) 20-12-027. VGIC agrees with the Joint Utilities that “there may be downstream adjustments
that the IOUs cannot foresee now.”2 The Advice Letter’s request for the resolution to “direct the
IOUs to comply with the most recent CARB regulation and guidance documents”3 represents the
most effective pathway forward to avoid unnecessary delays and conflicts. VGIC strongly
recommends that CARB’s new Pre-approved Holdback Projects not be improperly obscured by
conflicting guidance, as it risks undermining CARB’s explicit intent for the use of these funds. In
the case of any conflict, IOUs must comply with CARB’s language regarding the use of these
holdback revenues, as LCFS is designed and approved by CARB. This is the case not only for these
recently adopted amendments, but also future LCFS amendments that CARB may adopt. The

B. VGIC Supports Streamlining of LCFS Holdback Program Creation and Approval.
VGIC strongly supports efforts to streamline the process for submitting and approving
programs funded by LCFS holdback proceeds. Having holdback programs proposed and updated
on an ad hoc basis appears to create unnecessary friction in the processing of Advice Letters. This
has created administrative burdens and made it harder for stakeholders to track program
developments. VGIC agrees that having an annual filing process will help stakeholders better
evaluate utility LCFS portfolios and ensure more orderly and transparent review.
However, the benefits of this streamlined proposal process will only be realized if the
Commission also commits to predictable and timely approvals. VGIC respectfully urges the
Commission to adopt and communicate its timeline for approving both annual implementation plans
and annual forecast advice letters. Committing to approval by July 31st for implementation plans
submitted each January, and by December 31st for September forecast filings, would provide much-
needed regulatory certainty and reduce the bottlenecks that often delay program deployment. These
deadlines will enable utilities and partners to make timely investments that align with policy
priorities and grid needs.
VGIC also encourages the Commission to consider other methods of increasing transparency
around LCFS holdback programs. This could be via a webpage on the Commission website with
approved LCFS programs and pending Advice Letters or another central repository for information.
C. VGIC Encourages IOUs to Include Expected Multi-Year Budgets for Programs
Anticipated to Extend Beyond Two Years.
While the Joint Advice Letter proposes that IOUs include two years of budget in
implementation plans for new programs, VGIC recommends that utilities also include estimated
budgets for the full expected program duration when programs are anticipated to last longer than
two years. Even if only the first two years are formally approved, providing a forecast of future
years will improve transparency and support more effective long-term planning by stakeholders and
the Commission.
VGIC is supportive of annual budgets true-ups through the September forecast advice letter
to reflect actual LCFS credit generation and revenue availability. Including longer-term projections
upfront does not limit the flexibility to adjust budgets later; instead, it enables stakeholders to better
understand funding expectations, program scalability, and resource needs at the outset. This
visibility is particularly important for programs involving infrastructure development or requiring
significant customer outreach and onboarding.

VGIC supports the Joint Utilities’ proposal to establish an annual January 31 Tier 2 Advice
Letter deadline for new LCFS program proposals. A regular cadence should improve regulatory
certainty, streamline Energy Division review, and allow more orderly evaluation of LCFS-funded
portfolios. Because 2025 is a transition year, VGIC respectfully requests that the Commission clarify
that utilities may continue to submit—and Energy Division may continue to act upon—additional
LCFS program Advice Letters during 2025, prior to the first formal January 31, 2026 filing cycle.
Restricting Advice Letter activity this year could delay timely, high-value vehicle-grid-
integration initiatives that fall within CARB’s pre-approved Holdback Projects. VGIC therefore
urges the Commission to state that utilities may still file LCFS-funded proposals on an ad-hoc basis
in 2025 and that such filings will remain eligible for review and disposition outside the annual cycle
when appropriate.
Maintaining this flexibility will sustain momentum. VGIC recently supported Southern
California Edison’s Advice Letter 5536-E a Tier 2 advice letter that proposes a large-scale managed
charging program, the Orchestrated Charging and Advanced Resiliency for Distribution
(“ORCHARD”) Program, consistent with CARB guidance and Commission direction in Decision
20-12-027. VGIC encourages the Commission to ensure that Advice Letter 5536-E, and any similar
proposals that Pacific Gas and Electric or San Diego Gas and Electric may advance in the coming
months, can be processed without deferral to 2026.
