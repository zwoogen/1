In accordance with Rule 11.1 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California
Public Utilities Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”)1
hereby submits this response to the Motion of Enphase Energy, Inc. to Amend Scoping Ruling and
Schedule to Address Optional Flexible Connection Agreements (“FCA”) and the Underwriters’
Laboratory (“UL”) 3141 Standard for Power Control Systems (“PCS”) in Investor-owned
Utilities’ (“IOU”) Energization Rules (“Motion”) filed by Enphase Energy on May 6, 2024.

II. VGIC SUPPORTS ENPHASE’S MOTION TO INCLUDE POWER CONTROL
SYSTEMS, UL 3141, AND FLEXIBLE CONNECTION AGREEMENTS IN
TRACK 3 OF THIS PROCEEDING.
In recognition of the goals and challenges highlighted above, the Smart Inverter
Operationalization Working Group (“SIOWG”) in this proceeding determined that, “operational
flexibility [should be] the highest priority, namely the ability of the DSOs in the high DER future
to flexibly optimize the use of existing capacity, allowing more rapid connections of DER and
loads, while still maintaining grid safety and reliability.”2 VGIC agrees that this is the most
important smart inverter operationalization use case, particularly for the development of Flexible
Connection Agreements or Limited Load Profiles (“LLP”). While the report discussed leveraging
operational flexibility in “the high DER future”, many of these techniques and technologies can

and should be leveraged today or in the very near future to enable customers to energize load
more quickly and reduce ratepayer costs. As discussed by Enphase, the PCS that would create
operational flexibility is already widely used to control DERs and is a technology understood by
utilities.3 The UL standard that would allow PCS to be used for LLPs is expected to be published

With this in mind, it appears the current barrier to deployment is largely regulatory, since
the development of LLPs may require updates to utility rules and tariffs. Rules 2, 15, 16, 29, and
45 may need some modification and specific tariff language and terms and conditions need to be
developed to enable an LLP, just as was adopted recently for Limited Generation Profiles (“LGP”).
VGIC supports Enphase’s proposed question to be added to the Track 3, Phase 1 scope to address
these issues, “Should the investor-owned utilities be ordered to amend their energization rules to
allow customers to elect optional flexible connection agreements that use UL 3141-certified power
control systems to implement a Limited Load Profile that avoids distribution upgrades, thus
enabling rapid and cost-effective project deployment and operations?”5 We also agree that tariff
provisions and technical requirements for LLPs should be developed.
Development of these updated rules and tariffs will take time and conversations should
begin as soon as possible with workshops or other discussion since it requires significant utility
and stakeholder engagement to develop any necessary tariffs and programs. For example, the
SIOWG had meetings for two years to develop these report recommendations and the development
of the Limited Generation Profiles, which creates a flexible export agreement, took 3.5 years to
finalize. Therefore, while the updated UL 3141 standard has not been published yet, workshops

III. DEVELOPMENT OF FLEXIBLE CONNECTION AGREEMENTS SHOULD BE
CLOSELY COORDINATED WITH THE ENERGIZATION TIMELINE
RULEMAKING.
The Commission is currently looking to address difficulties connecting new load to the
grid by setting target maximum and average energization timelines in Rulemaking (“R.”) 24-01-
018. As VGIC expressed in comments on Phase 1 of that Rulemaking, the critical topic of flexible
service connection, which includes elements like FCAs and LLPs, will help the utilities achieve
reasonable timelines for customer energization.6 In the Energization Timelines OIR, VGIC
recommends that the utilities include reporting about whether customers were offered and/or will
use flexible service connections. Once FCAs and LLPs are developed, recording which customers
leverage these agreements should be included in the utility energization timelines reporting.
Additionally, reporting which factors are creating energization limitations, what is
lengthening the energization timeline, and data on which flexible service connection types and
technologies are most common can help to inform the ongoing development of FCAs and LLPs.
Therefore, VGIC recommends close coordination between both this High DER proceeding and the
Energization Timeline proceeding to ensure that the Commission can gather data on how flexible
service connections are used and how to leverage FCAs to create faster energization timelines for
customers.
