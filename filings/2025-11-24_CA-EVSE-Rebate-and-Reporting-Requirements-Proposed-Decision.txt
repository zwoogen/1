In accordance with the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”) hereby submits
these reply comments on the Proposed Decision Adopting Revised Data Gathering and Reporting
Requirements for Transportation Electrification Programs and Providing Clarification on
Programs Adopted in Decision 22-11-040 (“PD”) issued by President Alice Reynolds on October
27, 2025.
I. INTRODUCTION.
VGIC appreciates the opportunity to submit these reply comments on the PD. VGIC
supports the Commission’s efforts to refine transportation electrification (“TE”) reporting
requirements and ensure that customer-facing programs are designed and administered effectively.
In these reply comments, VGIC responds to opening comments from the investor-owned utilities
(“IOUs”) with the following:
• The Technical Assistance (“TA”) Program should be IOU-Administered with flexible
budgets and timelines.
1

II. THE TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE PROGRAM SHOULD BE IOU-
ADMINISTERED WITH FLEXIBLE BUDGETS AND TIMELINES.
In Opening Comments, the IOUs requested that the TA program be administered
individually by each utility rather than through a central program administrator, as originally
envisioned in D.22-11-040.1 VGIC agrees. The IOUs are best positioned to provide technical
assistance within their own service territories, particularly given that energization appears to be a
central focus of the program.2 As PG&E states, “Because the utilities are closest to the work of
energizing customers, they are uniquely equipped to deliver customer advising on topics related
to the grid, capacity, and site energization.”3
VGIC is also concerned with the PD’s proposal to limit budget availability to a three-year
period. PG&E and SDG&E both explain that expenditures will begin prior to launch for program
setup and will continue after program completion for closeout activities such as reporting and
evaluation.4 PG&E states that these program phases can last “several months,”5 and in VGIC’s
experience, this can easily extend to a year or more depending on program complexity. While
VGIC is hopeful that the IOUs can stand up these programs quickly, customers should not face
artificially shortened program availability simply because administrative phases take longer than
anticipated.
Instead, VGIC supports PG&E’s recommendation that “the Commission should authorize
TA program spending until each IOU’s allocation of the overall program budget of $36 million is
