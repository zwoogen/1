REPLY COMMENTS OF THE VEHICLE-GRID INTEGRATION COUNCIL ON
PROPOSED RULE NO. 15
Pursuant to the guidance provided in Procedural Order No. 1, issued February 13, 2026 by
Commissioner and Presiding Officer Tammy Cordova, the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council
(“VGIC”) respectfully submits reply comments on NV Energy’s Proposed Rule No. 15.
I. INTRODUCTION.
VGIC is a national 501(c)6 member-based advocacy group committed to advancing the
role of vehicle-grid integration (“VGI”) through policy development, education, and research.
VGIC aims to ensure the value from flexible EV charging and discharging is recognized and
compensated to achieve a more reliable, affordable, and efficient electric grid. VGIC’s members
include automotive original equipment manufacturers (“OEMs”), EV charging station
manufacturers, EV charging site developers, distributed energy resource (“DER”) aggregators, EV
charging station installers, operations, and maintenance providers, EV and charging station
hardware suppliers, and publicly-owned electric utilities.

II. THE PROPOSED RULE SHOULD BE REVISED TO CORRECT ERRORS
RELATED TO THE DEFINITION OF VEHICLE-TO-GRID (V2G) EV.
The Proposed Rule 15 would define “Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) EV” on sheet 93M as:
“An EV that has the bidirectional transfer of electricity between it and the Utility’s
Distribution System enabled through Certified bidirectional EVSE for V2G-AC, or
an onboard inverter for V2G-DC, and that operates in Parallel Operation with the
Utility’s Distribution System. V2G operation may include charging from the
Utility’s Distribution System, discharging to serve all or a portion of onsite Host
Load, and/or Exporting to the Utility’s Distribution System. For purposes of this
Rule, a V2G EV (meaning that its V2G operational mode is enabled) is considered
an ESD.”
This proposed definition is technically incorrect, as AC bidirectional charging systems convert
power onboard the vehicle, whereas DC bidirectional charging systems use an inverter offboard
the vehicle (e.g., in an EVSE or as a separate external wall box). Additionally, the definition
implies that V2G operation may include “charging from the Utility’s Distribution System,” which
is unclear, as EV charging is typically treated as load, not generation or energy storage. VGIC is
unaware of any other utility or utilities commission that has included or proposed to include
language in its definitions that deems EV charging “V2G operation,” including in California,
Connecticut, Maryland, New Mexico, New York, and Texas. With these errors in mind, VGIC
recommends the following alternative language be adopted:
“An EV that has enabled the bidirectional transfer of electricity between it and the
Utility’s Distribution System enabled through Certified bidirectional EVSE for
V2G-DCAC, or an onboard inverter for V2G-ACDC, and that operates in Parallel
Operation with the Utility’s Distribution System. V2G operation may include
charging from the Utility’s Distribution System, discharging to serve all or a portion
of onsite Host Load, and/or Exporting to the Utility’s Distribution System. For
purposes of this Rule, a V2G EV (meaning that its V2G operational mode is
enabled) is considered an ESD.”

Attachment 1 at PUC Sheet No. 93AZ(12) aims to detail various configurations and use
cases. The guidance details that a break-before-make resource is not subject to Rule 15. This is
consistent with VGIC’s understanding of Rule 15 and Rule 19. However, at PUC Sheet No.
93AZ(14), the guidance states that bidirectional charging systems using the same break-before-
make configuration are subject to Rule 15. The Proposed Rule 15 offers no explanation, nor is
VGIC aware of any precedent in other states, for applying this treatment for bidirectional charging
systems. Additionally, Proposed Rule 15 does not explain why this requirement is imposed on
bidirectional charging systems but not on other resources, such as stationary Energy Storage
Devices. Given the absence of a clear rationale for this requirement either in Attachment 1 or
elsewhere in the Proposed Rule 15, VGIC respectfully urges that the Proposed Rule 15 be revised
to clarify that these break-before-make bidirectional charging systems do not need to be subject to
Rule 15. With this in mind, VGIC recommends the following alternative language:
