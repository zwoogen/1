In accordance with Rule 14.3 of the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California
Public Utilities Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”)
hereby submits these Opening Comments on the Proposed Decision Adopting Dynamic Export
Rate Pilot and Dismissing Application for a Real-Time Pricing Rate Pilot (“PD”), issued in
Application (“A.”) 20-10-011 on September 25, 2023.
I. INTRODUCTION
VGIC appreciates the efforts of the Commission, Energy Division (“ED”) staff, and all
stakeholders in working toward a dynamic export rate pilot that is open to some San Diego Gas &
Electric Company’s (“SDG&E”) electric vehicle (“EV”) customers. While VGIC generally
supports dynamic export rates for EV customers, the rate design that the PD would adopt does not
2

represent a meaningful step toward California’s affordability and decarbonization goals. VGIC
emphasizes, as detailed throughout this proceeding by VGIC and the Environmental Defense Fund
(“EDF”), that the incomplete rate design, namely the lack of a distribution component, is highly
unlikely to incentivize customer participation. With this in mind, VGIC recommends the
Commission consider either of the two alternatives detailed in these comments and summarized
below:
• VGIC respectfully urges the Commission to revise the PD to direct SDG&E to adopt a
dynamic distribution component that leverages SDG&E’s currently implemented D-
CPP methodology.
• At a minimum, VGIC recommends the Commission reopen the record of this
proceeding to solicit additional evidence from SDG&E and other parties demonstrating
the fundamental participant economics for the proposed dynamic export rate.
II. VGIC URGES THE COMMISSION TO REVISE THE PD TO DIRECT SDG&E
TO ADOPT A DYNAMIC DISTRIBUTION COMPONENT THAT
LEVERAGES SDG&E’S CURRENTLY IMPLEMENTED D-CPP
METHODOLOGY.
As noted throughout this proceeding, VGIC expects no customer participation from vehicle-
to-grid (“V2G”) resources based on the proposed export rate design.1 VGIC acknowledges that
Electrify America’s status as a settling party indicates there may be some customer participation
in the export pilot, as detailed in the PD.2 This indicates participation from storage-backed
unidirectional charging facilities; however, that participation is by no means guaranteed.

Moreover, the V2G export use case – which is a separate set of participants with separate
costs and benefits – remains unsupported by the limited nature of the settlement rate design.
As highlighted by both VGIC and EDF throughout this proceeding, a distribution component, in
addition to the proposed marginal energy and generation capacity components, is needed to
establish a compelling rate for customers.
VGIC reiterates that the distribution component need not be designed from scratch.
SDG&E has already developed and implemented a Distribution Critical Peak Pricing (D-CPP)
adder in Schedules VGI and Public GIR. VGIC recognizes that SDG&E has concerns over the
feasibility of scaling its circuit-specific adder methodology (i.e., as used in Schedule VGI) in a
pilot with no customer enrollment cap. However, the alternative offered by the PD would lead to
the adoption of a rate that does not allow customers to offset the significant upfront and ongoing
costs needed to enable EV export in this pilot.
Additionally, SDG&E’s proposed export pilot is severely out of step with the
Commission’s CalFUSE vision and the Rate Design and Demand Flexibility Design Principles
adopted in D.23-04-040. The PD would, therefore, not only adopt a rate design misaligned with
the Commission’s efforts to promote EV exports but also ignore the opportunity to leverage
SDG&E’s existing rate design methodology that’s been proposed, Commission-approved, and
implemented three times (i.e., Power Your Drive VGI Pilot Rate, Power Your Drive Extension
VGI Pilot Rate, and Schedule Public Grid Integration Rate).
Notably, Pacific Gas and Electric Company (“PG&E”) recently proposed significantly
expanding their dynamic import and export rates to include a distribution component in recognition
4

of the need to unlock V2G exports to support the evolving electrical grid, including growing
distribution constraints.3
With this in mind, VGIC reiterates its concern about establishing a pilot that will not attract
customers and is fundamentally out of step with other Commission efforts to promote V2G and
dynamic export rates. VGIC believes the PD should be modified to represent a serious proposal –
a rate design that includes a distribution component to offer a more fair and complete export rate
– to promote EV exports.
III. AT A MINIMUM, VGIC RECOMMENDS THE COMMISSION REOPEN THE
RECORD OF THIS PROCEEDING TO SOLICIT ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE
FROM SDG&E AND OTHER PARTIES DEMONSTRATING THE
FUNDAMENTAL PARTICIPANT ECONOMICS FOR THE PROPOSED
DYNAMIC EXPORT RATE.
If the PD is not modified as recommended above, VGIC respectfully urges the Commission
to, at a minimum, reopen the record of this proceeding to solicit additional evidence from SDG&E
and other parties demonstrating the fundamental participant economics for the proposed dynamic
export rate. VGIC does not believe that SDG&E and settling parties have produced sufficient
evidence demonstrating that the proposed export rate described in the settlement agreement would
provide customers with net benefit over the two-year pilot period. For example, customers
participating using V2G chargers would be required to make significant upfront investments to
enable pilot enrolment. Notably, SDG&E’s previously Commission-approved V2G
demonstrations have yielded important cost data for V2G equipment planning, procurement,

VGIC believes that establishing a pilot that has not been assessed for whether customers will
find it worthwhile to participate may be an imprudent use of ratepayer funds and, in the case of
SDG&E’s proposed dynamic export rate, may only delay and obfuscate the critical goals
established by Senate Bill 676 and envisioned in D.20-12-029 (“the VGI Strategies Decision”).
IV. CONCLUSION
VGIC appreciates the opportunity to submit these opening comments on the proposed decision
adopting SDG&E’s proposed dynamic export rate pilot. We look forward to further collaboration
with the Commission and stakeholders on this initiative.
