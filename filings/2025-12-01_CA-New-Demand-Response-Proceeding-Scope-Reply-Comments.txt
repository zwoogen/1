In accordance with the Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”) hereby submits
these reply comments on the Order Instituting Rulemaking to Enhance Demand Response in
California (“OIR”), issued on September 29, 2025.
I. INTRODUCTION.
VGIC reiterates its support for the Commission’s goal of modernizing demand response
(“DR”) policy to reflect evolving customer technologies and grid needs. In opening comments,
several stakeholders detailed the opportunities for electric vehicles (“EV”) and associated vehicle-
grid integration (“VGI”) solutions to be considered within the scope of this OIR. VGIC offers the
following comments in response to party opening comments, summarized below:
• Bridge funding for demand response programs should be pursued prior to the next
demand response applications.
o Specifically, ELRP should be extended for EVs given the lack of other
opportunities for EV aggregations in California.
• Developing universal pilot sunset criteria is not well-suited for this proceeding
given wide variations in existing pilot goals, designs, and customer types.

II. BRIDGE FUNDING FOR DEMAND RESPONSE PROGRAMS SHOULD BE
PURSUED PRIOR TO THE NEXT DEMAND RESPONSE APPLICATIONS.
In Opening Comments on the OIR, the investor-owned utilities (“IOUs”) raised concerns
that the next round of DR applications is due November 1, 2026 and this proceeding is just kicking
off now. VGIC anticipates that many of the matters discussed in this proceeding, such as guiding
principles, dual participation policies, valuation methodologies and data systems, would materially
impact the proposals put forward by the IOUs in their next application. However, the OIR does
not propose having an initial decision on even guiding principles until Q3 2026, or as late as
September 2026, which Pacific Gas and Electric (“PG&E”) states is too late to be fully
incorporated by the IOUs in their November applications.1
Instead, all three IOUs request some form of bridge funding for 1-2 years to allow for the
delay in the filing of a full 5-year DR application, which VGIC believes is reasonable and also
prudent given the importance of the DR issues that will be discussed in this proceeding. The IOUs
have slightly different recommendations and proposed possibilities for approving this bridge
funding, but all recommend 1 or 2 years of bridge funding and to extend the filing of the next DR
application by at least 6 months.2 VGIC believes that considering 2 years of bridge funding for
simple program extensions is reasonable based on the proposed schedule set by the Commission.
It is also critical that the approval of bridge funding be done expeditiously. Southern California
Edison (“SCE”) therefore recommends that bridge funding be considered and approved in this
proceeding instead of a separate regulatory venue. San Diego Gas and Electric (“SDG&E”)
similarly notes that the regulatory process for bridge funding should be much shorter and truncated
compared to a full-scale application, and that “filing a full application for a single year is not a

practical solution.”3 As SDG&E notes, there was a full application process for 2023 bridge year
funding and this required significant time and resources from the Commission, IOUs, and other
stakeholders (including VGIC). VGIC agrees that this level of effort is not necessary for bridge

Importantly, bridge funding should be approved for the existing Emergency Load
Reduction Program (“ELRP”), as highlighted by SCE. ELRP has provided an important
opportunity for customers to contribute critical emergency resources to California’s DR toolkit,
including from their EVs. ELRP allows customers to provide emergency capacity both by reducing
their charging load and by providing bidirectional discharge from the EV to onsite load or the grid.
There is no other utility-administered DR offering in California that allows for bidirectional EVs
to participate in this manner, despite over 2.4 million EVs on the road in California and a growing
number of bidirectional EVs, including currently from Ford, Chevrolet, Tesla, Kia, Nissan, GMC,
Cadillac, Volvo, Polestar, and Hyundai, as well as nearly all electric school buses sold in the state.
As highlighted by SCE, “Extending ELRP (or funding a similar new program) through the bridge
period will provide continuity […] avoiding the loss of critical megawatts (MW) as the state
pursues its 7,000 MW load shift goal.”4 The Commission should ensure that any bridge funding
approved for the IOUs includes a specific extension for ELRP.

III. DEVELOPING UNIVERSAL PILOT SUNSET CRITERIA IS NOT WELL SUITED
FOR THIS PROCEEDING GIVEN WIDE VARIATIONS IN EXISTING PILOT
GOALS, DESIGNS, AND CUSTOMER TYPES.
In Opening Comments, the Public Advocates Office at the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Cal Advocates”) states that this proceeding should discuss the development of
specific sunset criteria for DR pilots, arguing that the Commission does not have a sufficient
mechanism to end underperforming pilots.5 VGIC disagrees that this should be pursued at this
time. Pilots are highly diverse and can handle a wide variety of different types of elements, from
technical pilots that test new technologies, program pilots that test novel program designs and
customer engagement strategies, to rate design pilots that look at the impact of new pricing, and
more. Given the wide breadth of pilots, it would be difficult to establish standard criteria for their
sunset. Additionally, the Commission already has opportunities to review pilot performance. Pilots
are approved with a specific budget, and the IOUs are only allowed to spend up to that amount,
guarding against excessive ratepayer costs. Pilots are also reviewed regularly, and for DR pilots
specifically, they are reviewed during the DR application, which occurs every 5 years. It often
takes significant time for pilots to be stood up too, so by the time these pilots are reviewed, they
are unlikely to have 5 years of operational data, instead only having a few years to consider.
Regardless, each pilot is unique, and it is unlikely that universal sunset criteria will be able to be
reasonably created. Therefore, the Commission should focus its limited resources on other key
issues detailed in the OIR and stakeholders’ comments, including those detailed in VGIC’s
opening comments.
