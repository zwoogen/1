In accordance with Rules of Practice and Procedure of the California Public Utilities
Commission (“Commission”), the Vehicle-Grid Integration Council (“VGIC”)1 hereby submits
comments on Track 1 Microgrid and Resiliency Strategies Staff Proposal submitted in
Rulemaking 19-09-009. Pursuant to Administrative Law Judge’s Ruling Requesting Comments
on Track 1 Microgrid and Resiliency Strategies Staff Proposal, issued by Administrative Law
Judge Colin Rizzo on January 21, 2020, VGIC timely files these comments on January 30, 2020
in R. 19-09-009. VGIC also filed a motion for party status in these proceedings on January 30,
2020.
I. INTRODUCTION

of electric vehicles (“EVs”) and vehicle-grid integration (“VGI”)through policy development,
education, outreach, and research. VGIC supports the transition to a decarbonized transportation
and electric sector by ensuring the value from EV deployments and flexible EV charging and

II. COMMENTS
VGIC commends the considerable effort made by stakeholders that presented proposals
in the workshop held on December 12, 2019. In particular, the VGIC would like to thank the
Staff forconsidering proposals that enableVGIsolutions for resiliency purposes, such as
“Interconnection Proposal 4: Allow the use of smart meters for electrical isolation”2.
VGIC believes that solutions like Interconnection Proposal 4 that leverage existing plug-
in electric vehicles (“PEVs”) for resiliency functions would represent a significant step forward
in establishing a market for products and services that can help mitigate the impact of future
poweroutages. As the Commission weighs next steps, VGIC recommends that special
consideration be given to the short-term opportunities to achieve overall resiliency goals through
increased provision and intelligent integration ofPEVs. Actions can be taken now to facilitate
vehicle-to-home (“V2H”) or vehicle-to-load (“V2L”) solutions, which may allow PEVs to
provide backup powerto islanded loads in the event of a public safety powershutoff (“PSPS”).
Some of these actions can be taken in the short-term to enable V2H/V2L implementation (at
least for a subset of PEVs), by the September 1, 2020 target date. For example, VGIC
recommends that the Commission requireeach IOU to establish a pilot program with the goal of
implementing V2H or V2L backup power solutions in advance of the target date. If successful,
theseprograms can be scaled up in future years. Furthermore, these programs would also serve

Given the current state of technology, including the capabilities of smart meters and
available PEVs, the VGIC believes that overlooking V2H/V2L solutions would not constitutea
comprehensive approach to the actions available in the short-term (i.e. before September 2020)

and EV supply equipment (“EVSE”) companies, some ofwhich are VGIC members or
supporters, have recently indicated thetechnological readiness of PEVs to provide V2H/V2L
functionality either through an on-board inverter (i.e. AC interconnection) orthrough an external
device (i.e. DC interconnection). While there are some existing regulatory barriers to the broader
implementation ofV2Gsolutions through an AC approach due to current interconnection rules
(i.e. Rule 21) and standards, VGIC believes these solutions could still be pursued now on a
limited pilot scale.3 Additionally, there are fewer barriers to V2H/V2L solutions through a DC
interconnection approach which could be readily implemented now.
VGIC would like to emphasize that V2H or V2L backup power solutions represent only a
subset of VGIapplications that could aid the deployment of microgrids and resiliency solutions.
As such, VGIC would like to continue discussion on pathways to deploying V2HorV2L backup
powersolutions more broadly, and beyond the 2020 target date, as well as explore other
solutions not specified above.
